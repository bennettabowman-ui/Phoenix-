## The situation, plainly

The last few years have been a test, and the next few will be another one.
Pandemics, banking wobbles, AI dislocation, weather events, platforms that
ban you with a single email, jobs that vanish in a quarter. None of it is
the end of the world. All of it is unevenly distributed, and the people
who do well in those windows are not the loudest preppers or the giddiest
optimists.

They're the ones who already had a plan they'd practiced.

That's what this is.

---

## What's actually wrong with most "preparedness" material

Two failure modes dominate the genre.

**One:** doomer theater. Bunkers, beans, identity politics, an aesthetic of
collapse. Useful for nothing except feeding the dread.

**Two:** sleek techno-optimism. "AI will solve it." "Just diversify your
income streams." Generic, frictionless, untestable, and quietly arrogant.

Neither helps you when the actual weirdness arrives.

What helps is a **system you have already used** — to map your real
dependencies, identify your fallbacks, rehearse the two or three scenarios
that would actually disrupt you, and make decisions you'll be proud of
six months later.

## What the Resilience Stack is

A practical, modular kit that walks you through doing exactly that, in the
order that works.

Eight modules, designed to compound:

- **The Resilience Operating System.** A short, opinionated essay that
  sets the frame: high agency over hoarding; decision quality over
  prediction; networks over fortresses.
- **The Capability Audit.** A worksheet that maps what you, your household,
  and your nearest network can actually *do* — alone, today, with no
  internet.
- **The Dependency Inventory.** A structured database template for every
  critical thing you depend on, the longest outage you'd survive, your
  fallback, and what upgrading the fallback would cost.
- **Five Playbooks.** Six-to-ten-page operational guides for the scenarios
  that actually disrupt knowledge workers and households: income halves
  overnight; a platform you depend on bans you; the internet flakes for a
  week; a household emergency; a local disruption (weather, grid, civic).
- **The Skill-Stack Planner.** A 90-day plan for picking up offline-
  deliverable skills, with honest disclaimers about what one weekend
  actually buys you.
- **The Prompt Library.** Around 80 carefully designed prompts for any
  frontier LLM — for decision-making, self-interview, drafting awkward
  comms, and structured red-teaming of your own plans.
- **The Quarterly Review Ritual.** A 90-minute practice that turns the
  whole thing into a habit, so you're not buying another digital product
  that gets opened twice.
- **Five printed Decision Cards.** Wallet-sized. One per playbook. They
  exist on paper because the things they cover sometimes happen on days
  the screens don't work.

> **Design rule:** every module works on paper. Notion is a convenience
> layer, not the core.
