# The system I'm releasing today

The most useful thing I made this year isn't an app or an essay. It's a
twelve-page worksheet I keep reprinting because every quarter it tells me
a different uncomfortable truth about what I actually depend on.

Today I'm releasing the system around it.

It's called the **Resilience Stack**, and it is the version of this work I've
been quietly trying to write for a long time.

---

## What it is

A modular toolkit for thinking clearly when things get weird — for
individuals and households who want a real plan, not a bunker, not a
podcast, not the assumption that AI will sort it out.

Eight modules, designed to compound. A capability audit that maps what you
can actually do alone, today, with no internet. A dependency inventory for
every critical thing you rely on, and what your fallback would cost. Five
operational playbooks for scenarios that actually disrupt knowledge workers
and households — your income halving, a platform you depend on banning
you, the internet flaking for a week, a household emergency, a local
disruption. A 90-day skill-stack planner. About 80 carefully designed
prompts for any frontier LLM. A quarterly review ritual that keeps the
whole thing alive six months in. And five printed decision cards, wallet-
sized, because the things they cover sometimes happen on days the screens
don't work.

Design rule for everything in it: **it has to work on paper.** Notion is a
convenience layer, not the core. The PDFs work without internet. The
prompts work on any model. Nothing in here is locked to a vendor that could
deprecate me.

## What it isn't

Two failure modes dominate this genre, and I want to be clear that the
Resilience Stack is not either of them.

It is not doomer theater. There are no beans, no bunkers, no aesthetic of
collapse, no political tribal signaling. I think dread is a terrible
operating system.

It is also not sleek techno-optimism. "Diversify your income streams,"
waved away with a gesture. "AI will solve it." Generic, frictionless,
quietly arrogant, useful for nothing on the day the actual weirdness shows
up.

What helps is a system you've already run through once. That's all this
is.

## Who it's for

A solo operator, freelancer, or knowledge worker who has noticed the world
is non-stationary and prefers a system to a vibe.

A household captain coordinating a partner, kids, or a small community.

A small co-op or study group that wants to do a structured quarterly
review together.

That's it. If you want bunker porn or end-times prophecy or a financial-
survival hype product, this is not your kind of thing, and I'd rather you
buy something else.

## What's inside, concretely

A one-click Notion duplicate template. A print-ready PDF, around 120 pages.
The prompt pack as `.md` and `.json`. Five print-ready decision cards.
A versioned README and changelog so you can see what's new each quarter.

Solo tier $49. Household tier $99. Quarterly membership add-on $79/year
(new playbook every quarter, updated prompt library, the operator-log
newsletter, the quarterly briefing). Group license for up to 15 members,
$299 one-time. Thirty-day refund, no questions. If it doesn't help you
think more clearly about your real dependencies, write back and you keep
the files and your money.

## The useful bit, even if you don't buy it

If you only do one thing this week, do this:

Sit down for thirty minutes and answer one question — *if my primary
income source disappeared tomorrow, what is my honest 90-day plan?* Not
the plan you'd tell a friend. The actual plan, with names and numbers.

If the answer is fuzzy, you've just identified the highest-leverage place
to spend an hour this quarter. That fuzziness is the thing the Stack is
designed to remove. Whether you buy it or not, doing that one exercise
puts you ahead of where most knowledge workers are.
